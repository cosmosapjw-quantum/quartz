## Theory R1–R10, preserved on GitHub

The authority is `theory/R01_QUESTION_AND_BASELINE.md` to `theory/R10_HANDOFF_TO_CODING.md`; ten incremental commits with archive SHA and exact remote HEAD readback. Chaslot et al. (2008), *Progressive Strategies for Monte-Carlo Tree Search*, https://doi.org/10.1142/S1793005708001094 precedes progressive unpruning. Core finite lemma (R5): if a fully allocated immutable root edge list is searched serially under monotone active-set masks `M0⊆M1`, and only the selection mask changes at a checkpoint with no pending virtual loss, root edge `(N_a,W_a,M2_a)` and prior `P_a` remain exactly unchanged at that instant, and newly activated edges had `N_a=0`. Backprop conservation `Σ_a N_a=N_root` holds for one root edge per completed serial selection. Proof is an induction on the completed iterations plus a scalar mask-store at checkpoint. R7 Wolfram and exact Decimal/Fraction crosschecked counterexample `P=(1/2,1/3,1/6)`, `N=(8,0,0)`, `Q=(2/5,0,0)`, `c=2`, showing that activating another edge can change the PUCT winner despite all statistics remaining unchanged. No utility monotonicity follows.

**Implementation distinction:** full legal candidate list and edge slab are preallocated. The operation enables existing edge selection, NOT inserting previously absent edges into the arena; setup cost of the full slab is included. The earlier study `COG03-EINSTELLUNG-REOPENING-12` discarded the initial root and restarted a fresh tree. The present study retains the exact same root and 8 visits, so it closes a real implementation/semantic gap.
