# R6. Adversarial validation and false-interpretation controls

Strongest threats to the new formalization:

1. **Zero-gain does not imply zero information.** A diagnostic that only sharpens probabilities within a region where the same terminal action always remains best has G=0 even when posterior entropy falls. Check no-info (identical outcome rows) and no-decision-change (partially informative but too weak) separately.
2. **Observed reward samples are not automatically calibrated computations.** In adaptive MCTS, shared-tree rollouts are correlated and tree policy depends on previous outcomes. Applying the finite-IID formula directly to visit counts would be implementation and theoretical transport error. C17 must remain a game-independent *reference*, not a silently deployed production controller.
3. **Information dominance is not net-value dominance.** Perfect state disclosure has larger gross EVSI than binary partial disclosure but may have higher cost; R4 shows gross/rate/net differences, not a universal failure of rate scheduling.
4. **Stop-rule nuance.** It is incorrect to infer that rate selection necessarily changes the myopic STOP decision. For κ>0, each inequality G/κ>λ ⇔ G−λκ>0 is exact. The counterexample targets **which** computation is selected when multiple have positive net value. Conflating these is a mathematical misreading.
5. **Cost units are not "just a parameter".** λ carries utility per resource. Replacing milliseconds with microseconds multiplies costs by1000 and requires λ/1000 to preserve the decision. A raw rate without units or fixed exchange rate cannot be interpreted as an overall normative law for a one-shot problem.
6. **Unobserved/zero-probability outcomes.** Bayesian update divides by q(y); skip q=0 branches, never invent a posterior or renormalize a zero-probability event to create false evidence. Reject invalid likelihood rows, negative/NaN costs and payoffs, and mismatched action/state dimensions.
7. **Ties, action labels and counterexample stability.** Branch selection is deterministic lexicographic under ties but attainable utility/risk is label-permutation invariant. Substituting a different labelling must not change G or V. Validate exact fractions separately from f64, never widen tolerance to pass wrong arithmetic.
8. **Information/decision model uncertainty.** If Θ,U,L are misspecified, R and G can be systematically wrong despite exact arithmetic. A false probability model may produce a seemingly optimal compute action that fails in a real domain. C8 will test only synthetic frozen examples with known laws; actual game/controller non-inferiority remains UNRESOLVED.
9. **Human signatures and game strength are separate falsifiers.** Even correct Bayes-optimal selection for a finite toy channel does not demonstrate QUARTZ THESIS P2 NN-evaluation saving, P3 human expert emergence, or production CPU hot-path gate P4.
10. **No invented novelty:** the meta-level Bellman equation and EVSI are classical. The evidence-backed present change is reconciling the original frozen project's semantics with a counterexample and implementation, leaving 26 foundry axes as historical candidates rather than multiplying gates.

Conclusion: adopt R5 as a valid conditional reference. Preserve the original frozen THESIS and annotate its *rate* as a heuristic until an explicit throughput/economic objective equates it to priced control. No new strong no-go theorem has been proved, so no "impossibility of QUARTZ" statement is permitted.
