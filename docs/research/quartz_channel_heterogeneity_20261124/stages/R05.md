# R05

Constructive counterexample (binary signal, U=0 or 1, equal prior, b=1/2). For U=0 choose R=P(Y=1) equally 1/10,9/10; for U=1 R=1/2. One signal has same likelihood for both U so H1 stops at 1/2 with positive cost. P(11|U=0)=41/100 vs 1/4 if U=1; P(10|U=0)=9/100 vs 1/4. E[U|11]=25/66 and E[U|10]=25/34. P(same)=66/100; P(different)=34/100. E max(b,E[U|Y1,Y2])=29/50. Each observation costs 1/100, therefore H2=14/25=0.56, whereas F with the same first signal law stays at 1/2. Information-only first FOCUS is valuable. More generally, Cov(1_{Y1=i},1_{Y2=j}|U,n)=Cov(R_i,R_j|U,n); positive diagonal variance means frozen likelihood cannot reproduce the repeat distribution. Under correct model expected repeat log-loss reduction equals I(Y1;Y2|U,n), conditional on U; it does not imply greater realized utility or positive net value. The covariance term is an explicit fluctuation of the observation law, not total latent utility variance.
