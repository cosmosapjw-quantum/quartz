# Primary source transfer
- Smith & Winkler (2006), The Optimizer's Curse: Skepticism and Postdecision Surprise in Decision Analysis, §2.3 Proposition1, https://jimsmith.host.dartmouth.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/The_Optimizers_Curse.pdf . Conditional unbiasedness of each estimate does not imply unbiased selected estimate; proof uses true maximum and estimated maximum. Our K19 excess can involve both model misspecification and selection; this proposition alone does not diagnose which.
- Russell & Wefald (1989), https://ijcai.org/proceedings/1989/0053.pdf , original complete-computation utility difference and single-step limitation reused from K19. Sezener & Dayan (2020), https://proceedings.mlr.press/v124/sezener20a/sezener20a.pdf , definition4 and proposition2 reused: Bayesian simple regret decline under corresponding belief law, not a guarantee for fitted policy.
- Schutte, Postek & Yorke-Smith (2024), Robust Losses for Decision-Focused Learning, §2 Eq1–3, https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2024/0538.pdf : empirical optimization regret vs expected regret, uncertain parameters. K20 direct gain estimates target fixed readout utility; no claim to implement their robust optimization algorithms.
- Iyengar, Lam, Wang (2023), Optimizer's Information Criterion, https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10081 , SciSpace abstract https://scispace.com/papers/optimizer-s-information-criterion-dissecting-and-correcting-3j27q2si : optimism under data-driven optimization and bias correction; no OIC theorem used.
- Kallus & Uehara (2020), https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/kallus20b/kallus20b.pdf : cross-fold nuisance estimation in off-policy evaluation, not our complete paired intervention atlas. We use disjoint readout fitting and acquisition fitting, not their doubly robust estimator.
