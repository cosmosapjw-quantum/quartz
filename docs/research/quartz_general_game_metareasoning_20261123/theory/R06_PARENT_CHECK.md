3. STOP proof. The query identity is determined before observing its value; conditional independence is required at exactly this step. Incumbents are nondecreasing and g_a is nonincreasing in b. There are finitely many openings, so no unproved optional-stopping/infinite-horizon assumption is needed. Under equality STOP is optimal, not uniquely optimal.
4. Capped-value proof. Subtracting all nonnegative excesses bounds the realized reward by the maximum of capped rewards. Independent opening indicators let expected charges equal expected subtracted excesses. Under reservation ordering only the final opened candidate can exceed its own threshold; its threshold bounds every remaining capped reward. Both bounds are attained. Negative z are valid, though with b>=0 they are never opened. Atoms and tied indices do not obstruct the proof.
5. Three-point formula. Check all support boundaries z=1/2 and z=0, p_1=0, p_h+p_1=0, certain win/draw/loss, and positive cost. Both neighboring branches agree; the all-loss case gives z=-d. 6. Budget and charge. All-negative one-step gain remains a sufficient STOP criterion under a cap. A positive feasible query gives the converse. Pandora's order is generally not optimal under a binding cap, as the explicit one-query example shows. TRAIN-predicted charge is not the fixed true charge of the theorem; record predicted/actual work and both model and realized net.
7. Gibbs. The unique variational optimum uses a fixed positive prior and tau>0. Additive common shifts cancel from choice. Positive rescaling requires scaling both lambda and tau. Finite tau can choose negative-net queries. A known b=1 removes all FOCUS via an explicit exact certificate mask; otherwise no silent Q<=0 mask is allowed. Local one-step Gibbs is not an entropy-regularized multi-step Bellman optimum.
8. Fluctuations. The Hessian annihilates the all-ones direction. Annealed and quenched quantities are different, and a computation's gain uses covariance of its posterior mean, not all latent covariance. Small-noise expansions are not uniform as tau approaches zero. A positive information gain can have zero decision gain.
9. Corrected dynamics. A rate W_ab transfers mass from b to a; its columns sum to zero. Detailed balance fixes the prior-weighted Gibbs law. Off-diagonal Lindblad jumps implement population transfer; diagonal jumps only dephase. Algorithmic relaxation time is distinct from measured runtime. If a Hamiltonian were introduced, its term would be -i[H,rho]/hbar; here H=0 and no hbar=1 or k_B T identification is assumed.
