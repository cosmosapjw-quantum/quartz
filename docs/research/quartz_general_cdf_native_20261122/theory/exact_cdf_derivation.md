# R03 | Distribution of posterior maximum, arbitrary proper Beta shapes

Let K independent unknown reward means θ_j, posterior θ_j|H_j~Beta(a_j,b_j), a_j>0,b_j>0, and proposed n_j additional Bernoulli draws independently conditional on each θ_j. Successes S_j|H_j~BetaBinomial(n_j,a_j,b_j), with p_j(k)=choose(n_j,k) B(a_j+k,b_j+n_j-k)/B(a_j,b_j). The *post*-computation posterior expected terminal utility of action j is X_j=(a_j+S_j)/(a_j+b_j+n_j), a discrete variable with support v_{jk}=(a_j+k)/(a_j+b_j+n_j) and mass p_j(k). Independence of distinct posterior arm means implies X_j are independent across arms, although within each arm posterior-predictive Bernoulli samples are NOT independent after θ marginalization.

The CDF of the selected postcomputation maximum T=max_j X_j is F_T(v)=∏_{j=1}^K F_j(v), where F_j(v)=sum_{k:v_{jk}<=v}p_j(k). Let V be the *sorted unique* union of all score supports, v_1<...<v_M. Then E[T]=sum_{i=1}^M v_i(F_T(v_i)-F_T(v_{i-1})), with F_T(v_0)=0. Gross one-step exact EVSI is G=E[T]-M0, M0=max_j a_j/(a_j+b_j). Under the correct model, 0<=G<=E[max_j θ_j]-M0<=1-M0. This holds even with unequal n_j, including n_j=0 (unobserved arm), and identical score values from different arms. Simultaneous updates at tied support MUST precede computation of the CDF increment, not create fake additional probability mass.

Compute all K(n_j+1) weighted score events, sort ascending by score (O(E log E)), sweep once while maintaining each F_j and their nonzero product and zero-factor count. Per event update F_j and product ratio Fnew/Fold if Fold>0; when Fold=0, decrement zero counter and multiply Fnew. F_T is zero until all F_j positive. Equal-support event groups produce one CDF jump. Overall O(E log E), O(E+K) memory. Floating arithmetic is approximate even when mathematical formula is exactly derived; exact rational comparisons are used by independent finite check for integer shapes. End-to-end CPU including loading, parsing, serialization and host bridging is separate from mathematical operation count.
